## See it in 30 seconds: a citation catches drift on its own

A specification cites a product rule by its ID and a content digest. Someone edits the rule. The next time `citations verify` runs, it marks the citation `stale`. No one had to remember to check.

**1. The spec cites the rule.** It records what it cited and the digest of that text at the time.

```yaml
# checkout-flow.citations.yaml — the spec's citation ledger
- id: BR-REFUND-001
  digest: sha256:9f2a1c…e7b4   # the rule's content when the spec cited it
```

`citations verify` reports `current`. The spec and the rule agree.

**2. The rule changes.** A product owner narrows the refund window.

```diff
# br-refund-001.md — the canonical product rule
- Refunds are accepted within 30 days of delivery.
+ Refunds are accepted within 14 days of delivery.
```

**3. The same command now flags the drift.** The rule's digest no longer matches the one the spec recorded.

```console
$ citations verify
BR-REFUND-001   stale   PRODUCT061   canonical content changed since the citation was recorded
```

The spec still says 30 days while the rule says 14, and now that gap is visible instead of silent. PDaC does not rewrite the spec for you: a human reviews it and updates the citation. `citations verify` reports one of four states for every citation:

- `current` — the cited text is unchanged.
- `stale` — the cited text changed since the citation was recorded.
- `tampered` — an embedded copy of the text no longer matches the original.
- `unresolved` — the cited artifact or anchor cannot be found.

The full rules are in the [Citation Contract](/spec/citation-contract/). The rest of this page explains the model that makes this possible.

## The bottleneck is moving left

AI-assisted engineering has made implementation fast. As implementation cost falls, ambiguity and decision quality become the larger share of delivery risk: the hard part is knowing, precisely and verifiably, what the product is. An agent that writes code in minutes amplifies whatever understanding it is given, including none.

PDaC models product knowledge as a versioned, validated graph of small, related Markdown artifacts: actors, journeys, use cases, business rules, domain language and requirements. Humans and AI agents consume the same canonical model, and every implementation increment traces back to the product knowledge it serves.

Spec-Driven Development tools own one implementation increment. PDaC owns accepted product intent: the graph that outlives every spec. Delivery owns its implementation choices within that intent, and what it learns flows back as evidence and proposed Product Changes. One-way authority, two-way learning. And the citation contract at the boundary is delivery-neutral: the same citations brief an SDD framework, an AI coding agent, or a human team working from the backlog. Give a frontier model verifiable citations to canonical product text instead of a ticket and you stop paying the re-derivation tax on every invocation; the ambiguity that remains becomes reviewable instead of invisible.

**Where this stands today:** the specification is a v0.1 draft under public RFC, weeks old and labeled accordingly, extracted from one working reference implementation. Independent conformance, a second implementation and external pilots are the next proof points, and they are release gates for v1, not assumed achievements. The [known limits](/known-limits/) page names every gap we know about.
